# 📈 Stock Market and Trading Basics

## 1. Major Stock Exchanges in India

India has two major stock exchanges:

1. **BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange)** — One of India's oldest stock exchanges.
2. **NSE (National Stock Exchange)** — A major stock exchange in India.

## 2. Types of Brokers

A broker is a registered intermediary that allows investors and traders to buy and sell financial securities through a trading platform.

### A. Discount Broker

* Offers a trading platform with generally lower brokerage charges.
* Usually provides self-service trading tools.
* Example: Dhan.

### B. Full-Service Broker

* Provides additional services such as investment research, advisory services, and personalized assistance.
* Charges may be higher depending on the services offered.

## 3. Watchlist

A **watchlist** is a collection of stocks or other financial instruments that a trader wants to monitor.

**Purpose:**

* Track price movements.
* Monitor selected stocks.
* Identify potential trading opportunities.
* Observe market trends without necessarily buying the stocks.

## 4. Types of Trading

### A. Equity Trading

Buying and selling shares of companies listed on a stock exchange.

### B. Futures and Options (F&O) Trading

Trading derivative contracts whose value depends on an underlying asset, such as a stock or an index.

* **Futures:** Contracts that create an obligation to buy or sell an underlying asset according to the contract's terms.
* **Options:** Contracts that give the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an underlying asset under specified conditions.

**Note:** Futures and options involve significant risks and can lead to substantial losses.

### C. Intraday Trading

Buying and selling a financial instrument within the same trading day.

**Example:** Buying a stock at 10:00 AM and selling it at 2:00 PM on the same day.

### D. Scalping

A short-term trading approach that attempts to profit from small price movements through frequent trades.

Scalpers monitor price charts and execute trades over very short periods.

## 5. Nifty 50

The **Nifty 50** is a benchmark stock market index of the National Stock Exchange of India.

* It represents 50 major companies selected according to the index's eligibility and selection methodology.
* It is used to understand the overall performance of a segment of the Indian stock market.
* Traders and investors use it to study market direction.

## 6. Liquidity

**Liquidity** refers to how quickly and easily an asset can be bought or sold without significantly affecting its market price.

### Example

* A highly liquid stock generally has many buyers and sellers.
* A less liquid stock may have fewer buyers and sellers, making it harder to execute trades at the desired price.

**Key point:** Higher liquidity generally makes entering and exiting trades easier.

## 8. Leverage in Intraday Trading

**Leverage** allows traders to take a position larger than the amount of their own capital committed to the trade.

### Why do brokers offer leverage?

* Intraday price movements can be relatively small.
* Leverage increases exposure to those price movements.
* It can amplify potential profits as well as potential losses.

### Example

Suppose a trader has ₹1,000 and uses 5× leverage.

* Own capital: ₹1,000
* Total position exposure: ₹5,000
* If the position rises by 1%, the gross gain is ₹50.
* If the position falls by 1%, the gross loss is ₹50.

These figures exclude brokerage, taxes, and other charges.

**Important:** Leverage magnifies losses just as it magnifies gains. It does not guarantee profits.

## 9. Types of Trading Orders

### A. Market Order

An order to buy or sell immediately at the best available market price.

**Example:** You place a market buy order for a stock trading around ₹100.

The actual execution price may differ from ₹100 because prices can change while the order is being executed.

### B. Limit Order

An order to buy or sell at a specified price or better.

**Example:** A stock is trading at ₹100, but you want to buy it at ₹95.

You place a buy limit order at ₹95. The order will execute only if the required price and market conditions are available.

| Market Order                    | Limit Order                                          |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Prioritizes execution           | Prioritizes price control                            |
| Actual execution price may vary | Executes only at the specified limit price or better |
| May experience slippage         | May not execute at all                               |

## 10. Candlestick Charts

A candlestick represents price movement during a selected period.

For example, on a **5-minute chart**, each candlestick represents five minutes of trading activity.

### Four Important Prices

Each candlestick contains four key prices:

1. **Open:** Price at the beginning of the period.
2. **High:** Highest price reached during the period.
3. **Low:** Lowest price reached during the period.
4. **Close:** Price at the end of the period.

### Green and Red Candles

* **Green candle:** The closing price is higher than the opening price.
* **Red candle:** The closing price is lower than the opening price.

If the opening and closing prices are equal, the candle's appearance depends on the charting platform.

### Remember

* Green = Close > Open.
* Red = Close < Open.
* The candle's body shows the difference between the opening and closing prices.
* The wicks show the high and low prices.

## 11. Technical Analysis

Technical analysis involves studying price charts, trading volume, and indicators to identify potential trading opportunities.

The two techniques in these notes are:

1. Exponential Moving Average (EMA).
2. Price Action.

## 12. Exponential Moving Average (EMA)

The **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** is a technical indicator that calculates an average price while giving greater weight to more recent prices.

### 9 EMA

The 9 EMA calculates an exponentially weighted average using the latest nine price periods.

On a 5-minute chart, the 9 EMA is calculated from the latest nine 5-minute candles, with exponentially decreasing weights applied to older prices.

### Why traders use the 9 EMA

* To observe short-term price trends.
* To identify possible dynamic support or resistance.
* To study how price behaves around the moving average.
* To help identify potential entry opportunities when combined with other analysis.

**Important:** Price touching or crossing the 9 EMA does not guarantee that the price will reverse or continue in a particular direction.

## 13. Price Action

**Price action** means analyzing price movements and chart patterns without relying exclusively on technical indicators.

Two important concepts are support and resistance zones.

### A. Demand Zone

A demand zone is an area where buying interest has previously been strong enough to support a price rise.

Traders watch demand zones for possible buying opportunities.

### B. Supply Zone

A supply zone is an area where selling pressure has previously contributed to a price decline.

Traders watch supply zones for possible selling opportunities.

### C. Support and Resistance

* **Support:** A price area where buying interest may slow or stop a decline.
* **Resistance:** A price area where selling pressure may slow or stop a rise.

Support, resistance, supply, and demand are better understood as price areas rather than perfectly precise lines.

## 14. Trading Strategy: 9 EMA + Price Action

The following is a simplified description of the strategy from these notes. It is an educational framework, not a proven or guaranteed profitable trading system.

### Step 1: Analyze the Previous Day's Daily Chart

Open the daily chart and examine the previous day's price movement in relation to the 9 EMA.

Use this analysis to develop a potential bullish or bearish bias.

### Step 2: Develop a Bullish or Bearish Bias

**Bullish bias:**

* Price is above the 9 EMA.
* The market shows signs of upward momentum.
* Look for possible demand or support zones on the intraday chart.

**Bearish bias:**

* Price is below the 9 EMA.
* The market shows signs of downward momentum.
* Look for possible supply or resistance zones on the intraday chart.

**Note:** Being above or below the EMA alone is not sufficient to establish a reliable trading signal.

### Step 3: Switch to the 5-Minute Chart

On the same trading day, open the 5-minute chart.

Identify:

* The intraday trend.
* Demand and supply zones.
* Support and resistance levels.
* Price behaviour around the 9 EMA.

### Step 4: Look for a Potential Entry

#### Possible Buying Setup

1. The broader chart suggests a bullish bias.
2. The price approaches a potential demand or support zone.
3. The price interacts with the 9 EMA.
4. A bullish candle or other confirmation appears.
5. Risk, stop-loss placement, and the potential reward are assessed before entering.

#### Possible Selling Setup

1. The broader chart suggests a bearish bias.
2. The price approaches a potential supply or resistance zone.
3. The price interacts with the 9 EMA.
4. A bearish candle or other confirmation appears.
5. Risk, stop-loss placement, and the potential reward are assessed before entering.

### Step 5: Manage Risk

Before entering a trade:

* Decide where the trade idea would be invalidated.
* Determine the stop-loss level.
* Calculate the position size based on the amount you can afford to lose.
* Consider brokerage and other transaction costs.
* Define an exit plan.

Never assume that a signal will always work.

## 16. Important Risk Reminder

Trading involves financial risk, particularly intraday trading and futures and options.

* Do not assume that a green candle means the price will continue rising.
* Do not assume that a red candle means the price will continue falling.
* Do not trade solely because the price touches the 9 EMA.
* Avoid using leverage without understanding the potential losses.
* Practise with paper trading before risking real money.
* Track your results and test your strategy on historical data before relying on it.
